What's actually in the rack

Lantern is built from a real, running setup. The parts are ordinary. The wiring is the point.

Illustrative diagram. Addresses, names and network details are deliberately absent.

  • Home Assistant on a Raspberry Pi is the one dashboard and the one automation engine. Every device, whatever its brand, shows up here.
  • Matter over Thread is handled locally: a Thread border router next to Home Assistant, so devices commission without a phone vendor's cloud in the loop.
  • Wi-Fi devices are reached over the local network through their integrations. Four vendor ecosystems, one place.
  • The NAS is where backups land and where the household's files live. A dead SD card is an inconvenience, not a loss.
  • Remote access is a VPN tunnel and nothing else. No port is open to the internet. No vendor relays your traffic.

Honest footnote: Lantern is a concept built around a real home network for a university course. It is not a product you can buy. The house in the 3D scene is a stylised home, not anyone's actual apartment.
